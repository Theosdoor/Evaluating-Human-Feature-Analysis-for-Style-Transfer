\subsection{Human Patch Extraction}

We use \texttt{YOLO26m} \cite{yolo26_ultralytics} to detect and extract 4000 human patches from the training videos.
The budget is divided equally between domains (2,000 each); within the movie domain it is allocated proportional to video duration so that longer clips contribute more patches. 
To prioritise diverse patches, we reject near-static frames and enforce a minimum spacing between YOLO calls (Table~\ref{tab:extract_params}).

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Extraction hyperparameters (empirically chosen).}
  \label{tab:extract_params}
  \begin{tabular}{lrp{6cm}}
    \toprule
    Parameter & Value & Role \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{yolo\_interval}, $\delta_f$            & 10 & minimum frames between YOLO calls \\
    \texttt{scene\_change\_threshold}, $\tau_{sc}$ & 8         & minimum inter-frame MAD; rejects near-static shots \\
    \texttt{blur\_threshold}, $\tau$               & 40        & Laplacian blur floor; normalised as $\min(\text{blur}/3\tau,\,1)$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Composite patch quality score components. Maximum achievable score is 0.75; sharpness is normalised against $3\tau$ (Table~\ref{tab:extract_params}).}
  \label{tab:score_weights}
  \begin{tabular}{llr}
    \toprule
    Component & Formula & Weight \\
    \midrule
    YOLO confidence & $c \in [0,1]$ & 0.30 \\
    Relative size & soft peak at 15\% of frame area & 0.25 \\
    Laplacian sharpness & $\min(\text{blur}\,/\,3\tau,\;1)$ & 0.20 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

We classify patches using a 3-layer Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) \cite{kipf2017semisupervisedclassificationgraphconvolutional, Yan_Xiong_Lin_2018} on COCO \cite{lin2015microsoft} skeleton graphs with node features $(x, y, \text{confidence})$, and confidence-weighted global pooling \cite{confidenceweightedpooling}.
We use \texttt{YOLO26m-pose} \cite{yolo26_ultralytics} to detect pose keypoints for the skeletons.
Table~\ref{tab:gcn_results} shows the results.

GCN training labels are manually annotated using a custom tool (Figure \ref{fig:annot_tool}) seeded with rule-based keypoint heuristics (Table~\ref{tab:rules}), which are less flexible than the learned GCN classifier. Horizontal flip augmentation (swapping symmetric keypoint pairs) doubles the GCN training set without requiring additional annotations.

Figure~\ref{fig:gcn_ablation} shows that manual annotations generally improve accuracy over rule-based labels, notably except for \texttt {Others} (Table~\ref{tab:gcn_accuracy}).

\subsection{Training Data Selection}


Many extracted patches are visually similar, so we select diverse training patches as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item DINOv2 ViT-B/14~\cite{oquab2023dinov2, darcet2023vitneedreg, jose2024dinov2meetstextunified}
          embedding of all non-\texttt{others} patches to cluster by visual similarity.
    \item Apply K-Means clustering in each class\,$\times$\,domain group. The 2,000-patch budget
          is split equally, with $k \propto$ group size
          within each domain.
    \item Select the centroid-nearest patch by cosine similarity (i.e. the most representative sample per cluster).
\end{enumerate}

Figure~\ref{fig:umap} shows the DINOv2 embeddings are not visually separable by class (left), but they are somewhat by domain (right). This suggests GCN skeletal features are more important than visual similarity.
